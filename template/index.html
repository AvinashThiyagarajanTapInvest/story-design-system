Tell the pitch as a story, not a deck

A short story in eight parts: why slides lose a room, what a story page is, how it is built, and what it asks of the people who make one.

01

Decks lose the room halfway through

The content was never the problem. The order, the proof and the pace were.

Where decks break

One idea per slide

The argument is cut into pieces, and the thread between them lives only in the presenter's head.

Proof is a screenshot

A blurry image of a chat or a dashboard, too small to read from the back of the room.

Real and planned blur

What works today and what is hoped for look the same, so the audience trusts neither.

Dead after the meeting

Without the presenter, the deck makes no sense to the people it is forwarded to.

Five things a pitch needs

Each problem above comes back to one of these five. A story page is built to cover all of them in a single file.

Narrative

One argument, in order, from problem to ask.

  • Chapters are numbered and read top to bottom
  • Every chapter title is a full sentence
Built in

Evidence

Proof rebuilt as live, readable mocks.

  • Chats, terminals and documents as real text
  • Charts drawn in SVG, never pasted images
Built in

Honesty

Real, planned and illustrative are always marked.

  • Live and planned pills on every claim
  • "Illustrative" on every made-up number
Built in

Pace

The presenter controls what the room sees.

  • J and K step one block at a time
  • Everything else dims, so nobody reads ahead
Presenter modeSpeaker notes

Reach

It still works after the meeting, on any screen.

  • Reads alone, without the presenter
  • Works on a phone, in light and dark
Built in
Story Pageone scrolling file that covers all five
02

It started with one pitch that had to stand on its own

The deck was going to be forwarded to people who would never hear it presented.

From deck to story

Step 1 · Done

One page

The deck became a single scrolling page, with the argument written out in full sentences.

Step 2 · Done

A system

The parts that worked became tokens, components and rules, so the next page starts from them.

Step 3 · Next

Every pitch

Any team can tell its story the same way, and readers learn the format once.

Once the first one landed, the question was obvious. What if every pitch read like this?

03

A story page is one file that reads top to bottom

No slides, no build step, no framework.

What it is

A story page is an argument you scroll, not a set of slides you click. The proof sits next to the claim it supports.

Technical and non-technical readers use it the same way: open the link and scroll.

Three parts

Part 1

Tokens

Colours, fonts, shadows and easing, with a light and a dark value for each.

Part 2

Components

Cards, mocks, charts, timelines and diagrams, each with one job and one entrance.

Part 3

Behaviour

Reveal on scroll, the chapter rail, reading progress and presenter mode.

How it works

The author writes one HTML file from the template. A script inlines the styles, and the file is shared as a single link that works in any browser.

What we learned

Watch for

Too many mocks

A scene per paragraph slows the read. One scene per point is enough.

Watch forTrust

Unmarked numbers

One made-up number without a label costs the credibility of every real one.

Worked

Reading ahead

People who read the link first asked better questions in the room.

06

Honest by design

Each layer stops a different way a pitch can mislead. They are in the format, not in a promise.

Live or planned, alwaysEvery capability carries a pill. Filled is working today; gold is planned.
Pills
Illustrative is labelledAny made-up number or mock carries "Illustrative" or "example" in its corner.
Labels
Sources in the footerThe last line of the page lists where every real number came from.
Footer
No private dataNo ids, tokens, emails, ratings or names of people who did not agree to appear.

Why it stays small

No build step

One file opens in any browser.

Tokens, not taste

Colour and type are decided once, for both themes.

Components, not layouts

Each chapter is assembled from parts that already work.

Checks, not hope

Three screenshots catch most layout mistakes.
